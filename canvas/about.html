How it works

One million pixels. Draw anything on the empty space, then decide whether to keep it forever or rent it for a week. When the canvas seals it gets printed at 2m × 2m and hung in public.

Claiming pixels

Draw first, pay after

Switch to Draw & claim, then draw anywhere that is still empty. The editor will not let you paint over pixels someone else already owns. When you are happy, the panel shows what your drawing costs, and you choose.

You pay for exactly what you drew

Not for a rectangle around it. If you draw a 40-pixel letter A inside a 100×100 area, you pay for 40 pixels — not 10,000. This is the part most pixel canvases get wrong.

Protecting the hole in your letter O

If your shape encloses empty space, someone could otherwise draw inside it. Tick protect the empty space inside and those sealed-in gaps become yours too. They cost the same per pixel, and you always see the number before you pay.

Forever, or seven days

Keep forever means your pixels stay for the life of Canvas #1 and appear on the printed version. Rent 7 days is a tenth of the price; when it expires the pixels return to the pool and someone else can claim them. You can renew or convert to permanent before it runs out.

Importing an image to trace

Drop any image onto the canvas, or pick one with the import button. Resize and position it, then either draw over it by hand at whatever opacity suits you, or convert it into pixels in one click and edit the result like anything else. You see how many pixels it would claim, and what that costs, before you commit — and the whole import is a single undo step.

The file never leaves your browser. It is decoded locally, and only the pixels you actually decide to claim are ever sent to us. That also means huge images are fine; only the size you place them at matters.

You need the right to use what you import. Artwork infringing someone else's copyright or trademark is removed without a refund, so please do not trace a logo you do not own.

Why there is a minimum payment

Card processing costs about 25 cents per transaction regardless of size. On a €1 payment that eats the entire operating share, so the minimum is €5. The per-pixel price is unchanged.

Renting, renewing, and keeping

A rental lasts seven days. Before it runs out you get a warning on the canvas with two options: renew for another week, or keep it forever.

A renewal is added to your current expiry, not to today — renewing early never costs you time you already paid for.

Converting to permanent is charged at the full permanent price. The rent you already paid is not credited against it. We would rather say that here than let you discover it at checkout.

If you do nothing, the pixels return to the pool and somebody else can claim them. Your artwork stays in the snapshots forever either way.

Territories

When you claim pixels you can tag them with a team: a country, a subreddit, a Discord, a university, a creator's community. Anything.

Every team has a live standing on the territories page, ranked by pixels held, showing how much each community has raised for the charity partner and how far ahead or behind it is. Rented pixels drop out of the standings when they expire, so a lead has to be defended.

Each team also has a share card that updates itself, so a community can post its current standing without anybody making a graphic by hand.

History

The canvas is snapshotted regularly and the history page plays those snapshots back in order. Snapshots are never edited.

That matters most for rentals: an artwork that lived for one week in week two is gone from the canvas but still in the record. When Canvas #1 seals, these frames become the timelapse, and the printed 2m × 2m canvas is the final frame.

Sharing

Every artwork has its own page at /a/<id> with a generated preview image, so a link posted anywhere unfurls into the artwork rather than a generic logo.

Any spot on the canvas is linkable too: panning and zooming updates the address bar, so copying the URL sends somebody to exactly what you are looking at.

For businesses

Any artwork can be made clickable for a flat €100. It is labelled as paid advertising wherever it appears, the link carries rel="nofollow", and it is reviewed by a person before going live.

The surcharge is treated exactly like the pixels: the same share of it goes to the charity partner. Advertising revenue is not carved out of the promise.

Links to malware, scams, adult content, or anything in the prohibited list below are removed without a refund of the surcharge.

Talking to other creators

Hover any artwork to see its title, who made it, and how long it has left. Click it for the full details.

Creators can publish an optional contact link with their artwork — a profile, a site, or an email address. It is there so people can coordinate about neighbouring pixels, trade space, or collaborate on something bigger than one person's budget. It is always optional, always public, and never a private inbox we operate.

Moderation

Every artwork is reviewed by a person before it appears on the canvas. Not after — before. This is slower and more work, and it is the reason a charity is willing to have their name next to a canvas that anyone can draw on.

Prohibited: illegal content of any kind; Nazi, extremist or terrorist symbols and propaganda; hate content; sexual content; sexual content involving minors; doxxing; credible threats; scams and fraud; copyright and trademark abuse.

Anyone can report any pixel — no account needed — and reports go into the same queue. Buying pixels does not buy the right to publish illegal content. If something has to be removed, permanent buyers keep their pixels and can draw something else; repeat or serious abuse ends the account.

The physical canvas, and the artist

When the canvas seals it is printed at 2m × 2m and hung somewhere the public can see it. Every pixel that was bought is on it. That is what "permanent" actually means here — not a promise about the internet lasting forever, but an object that exists.

A share of every payment also pays a local artist to hand-paint a companion version, filmed and published as a timelapse. The idea is that this project supports a working artist as well as a charity.

Because 2mm per pixel is far too small to paint by hand, the painted version works at block resolution: 100 × 100 hand-painted squares of about 2cm. That is a commission somebody can genuinely finish, which is why we promise that rather than a pixel-perfect hand copy.

Your money

25 cents of every euro goes to the charity partner, calculated on the gross payment before any of our costs come out. Every remaining cent — VAT, card fees, hosting, the physical canvas and its artist, the Canvas #2 fund, and what is left for the developer — is published line by line. In total 36% of every payment is committed before the developer sees anything.

Two numbers are always kept apart: money allocated to the partner, and money actually transferred with a receipt.

Questions people actually ask

Is this a tax-deductible donation?

No. You are buying a pixel placement, and we pass a published share of it on. We would rather say that plainly than imply something we cannot back up.

What does “permanent” really mean?

Your pixels stay for the life of Canvas #1 and appear on the printed canvas. If the project ever shuts down, the final image is published in full resolution and archived publicly. That is a promise one person can actually keep — “forever” on its own is not.

Can the canvas get bigger?

Never. A canvas that grows would shrink everything already bought. When Canvas #1 fills or its window ends, it seals and Canvas #2 opens as a separate thing.

Can the price go up if this gets popular?

Not while Canvas #1 is open. The price is fixed for the life of the canvas.

Can I get a refund?

You have the normal 14-day right of withdrawal for digital content, which you can waive at checkout to have your artwork placed immediately. If we remove your artwork for a reason that is our fault, you get your money back.

What happens to my artwork when it expires?

Rented pixels return to the pool and the artwork stops being displayed. It stays in the weekly snapshots, which are never edited.
