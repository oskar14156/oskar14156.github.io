2. The short version

Veil has no user account, no sign-in and no backend operated by us. We do not collect, receive or store your location. Your routes, your search history, your camera reports and your settings live on your device and are deleted with the app. Veil contains no analytics, advertising or tracking SDKs.

What follows is the detail, including the third parties that unavoidably see something in order to draw a map or calculate a route.

3. Data processed on your device only

  • Precise location (with your permission), to centre the map, plan a route from where you are and warn you about a camera ahead. It is used in memory and is not written to any server by us.
  • Recent destinations — up to twelve places, stored locally so you do not retype an address in a car. Clearable individually or entirely in the app.
  • Your camera reports — position, type and facing of a reader you add. These currently stay on the device and are not uploaded.
  • Camera and street data cache — a local database, including the plate-reader dataset bundled with the app.
  • Settings and free-tier counter.
  • Diagnostic log — a short, local, rolling record of technical events (for example "route planned, 12 candidates, 30 km"). It deliberately does not accept coordinates. Nothing is transmitted unless you tap Share diagnostic log and send it yourself.

4. What leaves the device, and to whom

Veil cannot draw a map or compute a route without asking someone. These are the only recipients, what each one receives, and why.

  • Apple (MapKit) — on iOS the base map and the initial route come from Apple. Apple receives the map area you are viewing and, when you plan a trip, the start and destination coordinates. Governed by Apple's Privacy Policy.
  • Routing engine — OSRM by default, or OpenRouteService if a key is configured. Receives start, destination and any intermediate points used to route around a camera. It receives no identifier and no account.
  • Geocoding — Nominatim (OpenStreetMap Foundation). Receives the text you type when searching, or a coordinate when you long-press the map to name a dropped pin.
  • Camera and street data — Overpass API mirrors. Receives a rectangular area (a bounding box), never a point and never your position. Note that a small area around you is still coarse location information.
  • Purchases — Apple processes the payment; we never see card details. If in-app purchases are enabled, RevenueCat is used to validate the receipt and receives a randomly generated, anonymous app-user identifier plus purchase status. It receives no location and no name.

These requests carry no account, no advertising identifier and no persistent user ID from us. But a destination coordinate is meaningful data: the operators above will see it, along with your IP address, as any internet request implies. If that matters to you, a VPN is the appropriate control, and Veil works normally behind one.

5. Purposes and legal bases (GDPR Art. 6)

  • Providing the app you asked for — showing the map, planning a route, warning about a camera ahead: performance of a contract, Art. 6(1)(b).
  • Location access — your consent through the iOS permission prompt, Art. 6(1)(a), withdrawable at any time in iOS Settings.
  • Purchase validation and fraud prevention — contract and legitimate interests, Art. 6(1)(b) and (f).
  • Local diagnostics — legitimate interest in a working product, Art. 6(1)(f); it stays on your device unless you send it.

6. International transfers

The providers above may process data outside the EEA, including in the United States. Transfers rely on the mechanisms those providers publish, such as EU Standard Contractual Clauses and, where applicable, the EU–US Data Privacy Framework. We do not transfer anything ourselves, because we receive nothing.

7. Retention

We retain nothing, because nothing reaches us. On-device data lives until you delete it or uninstall the app; the diagnostic log is capped and overwrites itself. Support emails you send are kept only as long as needed to answer them. Third-party retention is governed by the policies linked in section 4.

8. Your rights

You have the rights to access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection under the GDPR, and the equivalent rights under other applicable law. Because we hold no personal data about you, we usually cannot identify any data to act on — but you can exercise every one of these directly and immediately on the device: see Your data. For anything else, email oskarpolster.business@gmail.com. You may also complain to a supervisory authority; in Austria that is the Datenschutzbehörde.

Residents of California and other US states with comprehensive privacy laws: we do not sell or share personal information, and we do not process it for cross-context behavioural advertising. There is nothing to opt out of, because there is no collection.

9. Children

Veil is not directed at children and requires a driving context to be useful. We do not knowingly process data of children under 16.

10. Permissions

  • Location — while using the app: required to centre the map, route from your position and warn you about an upcoming camera. Denying it leaves the map and search usable; live warnings are not.
  • Veil requests no contacts, photos, microphone, camera or health access.

11. Website and changes

This site is hosted on GitHub Pages, which records server logs including IP addresses for security and abuse prevention. The site sets no cookies and embeds no trackers. If this policy changes materially, the date above changes and the app links to the current version.

2. Die Kurzfassung

Veil hat kein Benutzerkonto, keine Anmeldung und keinen von uns betriebenen Server. Wir erheben, erhalten und speichern deinen Standort nicht. Routen, Suchverlauf, deine Kamerameldungen und Einstellungen liegen auf deinem Gerät und werden mit der App gelöscht. Veil enthält keine Analyse-, Werbe- oder Tracking-SDKs.

3. Daten, die nur auf dem Gerät verarbeitet werden

  • Genauer Standort (mit deiner Erlaubnis), um die Karte zu zentrieren, ab deiner Position zu routen und vor einer Kamera zu warnen. Er wird im Arbeitsspeicher genutzt und von uns nirgends gespeichert.
  • Zuletzt gesuchte Ziele — bis zu zwölf Orte, lokal gespeichert. Einzeln oder komplett löschbar.
  • Deine Kamerameldungen — Position, Art und Blickrichtung. Sie bleiben derzeit auf dem Gerät und werden nicht hochgeladen.
  • Kamera- und Straßendaten-Cache — eine lokale Datenbank samt des mitgelieferten Datensatzes.
  • Einstellungen und der Zähler für die Gratis-Routen.
  • Diagnoseprotokoll — ein kurzer, lokaler, rollierender Verlauf technischer Ereignisse. Koordinaten werden bewusst nicht angenommen. Es wird nichts übertragen, außer du tippst selbst auf Diagnoseprotokoll teilen.

4. Was das Gerät verlässt — und an wen

Ohne Anfrage an Dritte lässt sich weder eine Karte zeichnen noch eine Route berechnen. Das sind alle Empfänger:

  • Apple (MapKit) — auf iOS kommen Basiskarte und Erstroute von Apple. Apple erhält den betrachteten Kartenausschnitt und bei einer Planung Start- und Zielkoordinaten. Es gilt die Apple-Datenschutzrichtlinie.
  • Routing-Engine — standardmäßig OSRM, alternativ OpenRouteService. Erhält Start, Ziel und Zwischenpunkte für Kameraumwege. Ohne Kennung, ohne Konto.
  • Geocoding — Nominatim (OpenStreetMap Foundation). Erhält deinen Suchtext oder eine Koordinate beim Setzen einer Stecknadel.
  • Kamera- und Straßendaten — Overpass-API-Spiegel. Erhalten ein Rechteck (Bounding Box), nie einen Punkt und nie deine Position. Ein kleines Gebiet um dich herum ist dennoch eine grobe Standortangabe.
  • Käufe — die Zahlung wickelt Apple ab; Kartendaten sehen wir nie. Sind In-App-Käufe aktiv, prüft RevenueCat den Beleg und erhält eine zufällige, anonyme App-Nutzer-Kennung sowie den Kaufstatus — keinen Standort, keinen Namen.

Diese Anfragen tragen kein Konto, keine Werbe-ID und keine dauerhafte Nutzerkennung von uns. Eine Zielkoordinate ist trotzdem eine aussagekräftige Angabe: Die genannten Betreiber sehen sie samt deiner IP-Adresse, wie bei jeder Internetanfrage. Wem das zu viel ist, für den ist ein VPN das passende Mittel — Veil funktioniert dahinter normal.
