2. What Veil does

Veil shows the locations of fixed traffic-surveillance cameras — primarily automatic licence plate readers — from public, openly licensed geographic data, and proposes a driving route that passes fewer of them. It requires no account.

3. Safe and lawful use

Veil is a privacy tool, not a tool for evading law enforcement. Every traffic law applies to you exactly as before. Do not operate the app while driving; set your destination before you move off, and follow spoken guidance only when it is safe to do so. Choosing which public road to drive on is lawful; using Veil does not make an unlawful act lawful, and you remain solely responsible for how you drive.

You must not use Veil to harass, stalk or locate a person, to interfere with an investigation, or to commit or conceal any offence. Do not abuse or overload the third-party data services the app depends on, evade the paywall, or reverse engineer the app except where law expressly permits.

4. Data accuracy — read this one

The camera data is incomplete and sometimes wrong. It is contributed by volunteers to OpenStreetMap. Cameras are added, moved and removed without notice; a reader may be missing from the map, may be recorded facing the wrong way, or may no longer exist. A route Veil describes as passing zero cameras may still pass cameras that nobody has mapped.

Veil is offered as an informational aid on a best-effort basis. It is not a guarantee of privacy, of accuracy, or of any particular outcome. Do not rely on it where being wrong would matter to you.

Route geometry, travel times and turn instructions come from third-party routing services and inherit their errors. Always compare against what you can actually see on the road, and never follow an instruction into a manoeuvre that is unsafe or prohibited.

Veil zeigt Standorte ortsfester Verkehrsüberwachungskameras — vor allem automatischer Kennzeichenleser — aus öffentlichen, offen lizenzierten Geodaten und schlägt eine Fahrtroute vor, die an weniger davon vorbeiführt. Ein Konto ist nicht erforderlich.

4. Datengenauigkeit — bitte lesen

Die Kameradaten sind unvollständig und teilweise falsch. Sie stammen von Freiwilligen aus OpenStreetMap. Kameras kommen hinzu, werden versetzt und verschwinden ohne Ankündigung; ein Leser kann fehlen, mit falscher Blickrichtung erfasst sein oder nicht mehr existieren. Eine Route, die Veil mit null Kameras ausweist, kann an nicht kartierten Kameras vorbeiführen.
